01 — Brand Essence

Classic craft, modern proof.

Jukuto digitises the Dojo — the manufacturing tradition of training an operator to mastery before they touch a real part — and adds the one thing the physical Dojo never could: measurable, auditable proof of competence.

The name

Jukuto is drawn from the Japanese juku (塾) — an intensive school of skill and mastery, a sibling to the dojo. It carries the heritage of the craft while sounding modern, global, and easy to say on a plant floor in Lucknow or a boardroom anywhere.

Positioning

The digital evolution of the manufacturing Dojo — not a replacement for the method, but its next form: scalable across sites, measurable per operator, and audit-ready by design.

Brand attributes

Reliable

Built on proven Dojo principles and enterprise-grade rigour.

Precise

Modern

Rooted in craft, mastery, and the discipline of the Dojo.

02 — Logo

The Measured Ensō.

An open ring — the ensō of the Dojo, a symbol of practice and mastery — closed by a single vermillion node: the checkpoint, the proof, the moment competence is confirmed. Craft and measurement in one mark.

Primary — horizontal lockup
Reversed — for indigo / photography
Logomark — app / avatar
On vermillion — node inverts to indigo
Favicon / app icon

Clear space & minimum size

  • Keep clear space around the logo equal to the diameter of the ring on all sides.
  • Minimum width: 28 mm in print, 120 px on screen for the horizontal lockup.
  • Below that, use the logomark alone.

Misuse — never

  • Recolour the ring or node outside the palette, or add gradients or shadows.
  • Stretch, rotate, or close the ring's opening — the gap is the point.
  • Set the wordmark in any face other than Fraunces, or place the logo on a busy, low-contrast background.
03 — Colour

Vermillion is the brand.

One colour carries the identity: a vivid Shu vermillion — the red of temple gates and maker's seals — used boldly and without apology. Deep Ink Indigo anchors it; electric Cobalt sparks the moments of data and interaction. No beige, no muted neutrals — crisp white does the resting.

05 — Motif & Iconography

The checkpoint line.

Beyond the logo, the brand speaks in one visual language: the measured line — a run of evenly spaced ticks, like a gauge or a sequence of checkpoints, with the active one in vermillion. Use it for dividers, progress, and section markers.

Checkpoint line — progress toward mastery, active checkpoint in Shu

Iconography

  • Line icons only: 2px stroke, rounded caps, geometric — matching the ring.
  • Indigo by default; a single vermillion accent where an icon marks status or completion.
  • No filled, skeuomorphic, or multi-colour icons.

Imagery

  • Real plants, real operators, real headsets — documentary, not stock-slick.
  • Bold, high-contrast grade with clean shadows; vermillion props or lighting welcome.
  • Duotone indigo or indigo-to-vermillion treatment for hero and section backgrounds.
06 — Voice & Tone

Proof over promises.

Jukuto sounds like a master operator, not a marketer: confident, precise, and plain-spoken. We lead with outcomes and numbers, respect the reader's expertise, and never oversell.

Precise

Name the metric. "40% faster to line-ready," not "dramatically better."

Grounded

Speak the plant's language — cycle time, scrap, IATF — not tech hype.

Quietly confident

Short sentences. No exclamation marks. Let the result carry the weight.

Do write

"Train operators to full competence before they touch a real part — and hand your auditor the evidence."

"Zero real parts. Zero line time. One per-operator log."

Don't write

"Revolutionary, cutting-edge XR that transforms your workforce!"

"The ultimate next-gen synergy platform for Industry 4.0."
